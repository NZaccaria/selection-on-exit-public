# Selection on Exit and Sticky Prices

Niccolò Zaccaria, Tilburg University

This version: August 2026

## Abstract

I study how selection on exit affects the transmission of monetary shocks. Using Dutch product-level scanner data, I document that low-sales products are more likely to exit the market and adjust their prices less often. I develop a menu-cost model with state-dependent exit, and I use it to simulate a counterfactual in which the total exit rate is unchanged, but turnover is random. Selection amplifies the real effects of aggregate nominal shocks: the composition of entry and exit raises the cumulative output response by about 15 percent, and the amplification reaches almost 35 percent in low-inflation regimes. Selective exit falls on firms whose price corrections were already near and spares stale prices elsewhere in the cross-section. At the same time, high exit risk shortens firms' horizons and weakens their incentive to adjust prices. The same mechanisms work under time-dependent pricing, but they mostly offset and leave a smaller amplification.
